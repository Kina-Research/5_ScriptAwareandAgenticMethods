\begin{abstract}
Large Language Models (LLMs) exhibit persistent performance gaps on morphologically rich,
low-resource languages such as Amharic, whose Ge\textquotesingle ez (Fidel) script and highly
agglutinative grammar are poorly served by standard multilingual tokenizers.  This paper
proposes a two-pronged framework that addresses these deficiencies through \emph{Ge\textquotesingle ez
script awareness} and \emph{agentic text processing}.  The Script-Aware model introduces a
character-level convolutional branch over a dedicated 500-symbol Fidel vocabulary, fused with
subword transformer representations to recover fine-grained phonological and morphological
signals.  The Agentic processor wraps this encoder in a three-step orchestration pipeline
comprising morphological segmentation, semantic disambiguation, and coreference resolution,
enabling iterative, structured reasoning over Amharic input prior to final inference.
Experiments on a purpose-built corpus of 5{,}000 Amharic instances spanning translation,
morphological analysis, and ambiguity/coreference resolution demonstrate consistent and
significant gains: the Agentic model achieves a BLEU score of 0.21 (+75\,\% over the
multilingual baseline), morphological accuracy of 0.74 (+27.6\,\%), and an ambiguity F1 of
0.72 (+30.9\,\%).  Ablation analysis confirms that the two paradigms are complementary,
with script awareness providing foundational orthographic literacy and agentic reasoning
supplying higher-level cognitive inference.  The results establish a principled, modular
pathway for advancing conversational intelligence in Amharic and other Ge\textquotesingle ez-script
languages.
\end{abstract}
